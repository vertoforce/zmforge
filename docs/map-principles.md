# Map design principles

These are the things every zmforge map should get right, written so a person or an AI reviewer can judge a map against them. `zmforge review <recipe>` measures and renders the map and writes a review packet; a person or any AI agent grades each room and the whole map against this list ([workflows/review-a-map.md](workflows/review-a-map.md)), and `zmforge review --import` checks and records the grades. The review is advisory: the hard rules live in the blueprint checks (Z-codes, [playable-map-requirements.md](playable-map-requirements.md)).

One principle per line. The id is what the review's JSON uses; "Judge" says what evidence settles it.

- **P1 Strong theme.** Every room and area has a strong, specific theme and nothing reads as plain: no bare concrete boxes, no default textures, no empty stretches. Every area is a scene: a one-line "what happened here" declared with `dressing.declare_scene`, which picks its set pieces, damage, fire and props ([map-design-method.md](map-design-method.md); check `S1`). *Judge:* room materials, fixture notes and prop models all say the same specific place (a DMV waiting room, a cemetery shed); a room whose walls/floor are generic and whose props could be anywhere fails.
- **P2 Dense, varied dressing.** At least ~100 props per indoor room, more for a bigger floor (a quarter of stock, 287 per 1,000,000 square units, in a room under 500,000 square units; 40% of stock, 459 per 1,000,000, in a bigger one, so about 460 in a 1000 x 1000 room); this is a floor, not a band, and more is better. Big outdoor areas carry far more (a whole map should carry thousands of props: tree lines, foliage, crops and clutter covering bare walls and open ground). The only upper limits are the engine's: a padded zm_corn_maze build of 39,975 props is the largest proven to link (probed; frame rate unmeasured), and only the first 340 shadowing lamps keep their shadow. Also clutter on surfaces (desks, shelves, counters carry things), and variety rather than one model repeated. Stock is far denser (zm_giant, per 1000 x 1000 units: 1,148 props not counting kit trim, 19% of them above ground, 399 mid and 113 overhead, counted from the shipped map); aim for it at ground, mid and overhead height, with set pieces splitting lanes to a median of about 256 units. `S2` warns under a quarter of stock (40% in a room of 0.5 k2 or more) or with nothing above ground; `S3` warns on a big room with no set piece; `S5` on a big roofed room with under 50 overhead props per k2; `S6` on a big roofed room with a wall run over 256 units that is one flat material floor to ceiling (`dressing.walldetail`). Floor litter is seasoning, not the meal: keep it under about a quarter of a room's props and in believable drifts (corners, under seats, by bins), never scattered evenly like confetti; the count must come from real furniture and set pieces. *Judge:* scorecard `counted_props` against `density_floor` (`counted_props` caps floor litter at a quarter of the count, so `props` padded with litter does not pass; `litter_share` shows how much of the room is litter) (`density_band` is `under` or `in`; never mark a room down for having more), the map's `engine_limits`, `on_surfaces`, `distinct_models`, `repetition_top_share` (above ~0.3 is repetitive unless the repetition is the point, like chairs in a waiting room or corn in a maze).
- **P3 Natural layout.** Furniture is laid out like a real room: groupings (desk + chair + lamp), some pieces in the middle, not everything shoved against the walls; nothing clips into or through walls, floats, or blocks doorways and walkways; every door, buy, perk and quest spot in a room stays reachable on foot (the Z55 route check). No zombie-proof camping spots: every place a player can stand, a zombie can reach. Clever spots stay: the fix is to give the zombies a way up (a stock `jump_up_N` traversal node pair onto the top), and an invisible clip cap only where no stock traversal fits. That means every top a player jumps onto (crate, sarcophagus, pew, stair rail, low wall) and every pocket behind or between furniture, not only the floors: `Z46` compares the player flood (jump 39, no mantle) with the walker flood (step 18, no jump) over every brush and solid-prop collision box, and errors on any spot no walker reaches and no walker can hit from within `ZM_MELEE_DIST` 64. What it cannot see: a spot reached only by a running leap, walls between a zombie and the player (a zombie within 64 through a wall counts as a hit), sloped brushes as surfaces, props with no measured box. *Judge:* `wall_hug_share` and `middle_share`, `through_wall`, `floating`, `solid_near_doorway`, the Z44/Z53/Z54/Z55 counts, and the prop table's positions and `sits_on`.
- **P4 Gags land.** Every joke or gag is obvious to the player: they see or are told exactly what happened by an on-screen line, rather than a joke that only works if they happen to read a prompt. *Judge:* each notice has a centre-screen reply that states the joke; gag lines explain the event; a joke that lives only in a prompt, or that refers to something the player cannot see, warns.
- **P5 Interactables say so.** Everything the player can use says to press the use key. *Judge:* every prompt contains `[{+activate}]` (the scorecard's text issues list the misses); a locked prompt names the key too when pressing it says anything (`quest.with_use_key`, `Z72`).
- **P6 A real quest.** Skill steps (shoot, knife, collect) run against a visible time limit once started, so they are a challenge while fighting zombies, and reset with a clear message on failure. A quest or Easter egg with varied step types (shoot, knife, collect/deliver, defend, ride, stand, kill-in-zone), the current task always named on screen (a short, flavourful description of the challenge, not step-by-step directions; see P17), hints that nudge without giving the place away, at least one mini-boss, and a themed buyable ending around 20,000 points. *Judge:* the quest facts: step types, task/hint strings, unlock rounds, boss config, exit price and win text. Timers, progress counts ("3/5") and failure/reset lines must be there; a vague-but-clear objective ("Shoot out the jack-o'-lanterns") is a pass, not a warning (P17). **Every step finishes or resets cleanly whatever else happens** (broken steps are the most common complaint on top Workshop maps): a dog round (timed and hold steps pause or, with `lockdown`, hold special rounds back and do not start during one), the player doing it going down, bleeding out or leaving (a carried item goes back to its spot), a player joining, the game ending. No required step rests on luck (random rolls, the box, random drops). Bosses are fair: a declared time to kill with the guns players have by then, normal spawns eased during the fight, co-op scaled by the players present. Checks: `Q1`-`Q3`, `Q6`, `Q7` (`verify/quest_safety.py`).
- **P7 Zombies essentials.** The standard pieces are present and sensible: perks, wall buys, mystery box, Pack-a-Punch, power, doors, zombie spawns in every zone, barriers. No perk limit, ever: a player may buy every machine on the map; the emitter lifts the stock cap of 4 on every map and `Z82` fails a script that keeps it. *Judge:* the essentials list, spawns and barriers per zone; a missing piece fails, an odd one (Pack-a-Punch in the start room, power behind the door it powers) warns.
- **P8 Progression makes sense.** You can reach everything, costs are sensible and rise as you go, and nothing is needed before it is reachable. Challenges and quest stations are staggered: one per room, each a door or two further on than the last, so players meet them one at a time instead of rushing them all at once. No challenge starts, shows its task or runs a timer before players can reach it: a quest step behind a door waits for that zone to open (zm_dmv's Vision Test ran its timer with the eye exam room still shut; `Z61`). The same goes for timed callouts that are not quest steps: a ticket call to a window behind unbought doors waits for that zone (`quest.gate_callout`). And a zone gate only means something if the zone starts shut: `init_zones` lists only the starting zone and zones no door leads into (listing every zone there opens them all at load; `Z67`). "Every player" counts only players present (`sessionstate` "playing"), clears its flag when the step opens and names who is missing (`quest.EveryPlayerFlag`). *Judge:* the door graph's cheapest cost into each zone, electric doors against where the power switch is, quest unlock rounds against the rooms their triggers are in, and quest point reachability.
- **P9 Consistency.** Names, jokes, on-screen text and props agree with each other (a sign that mentions a window has that window; a form named in a step is the one the player picks up), with no typos and no leftover placeholder text. *Judge:* read every string against the room it is in and the quest; the scorecard's text issues flag candidates.
- **P10 Nothing strange.** Anything else a player would find confusing, broken or pointless: a trigger nobody can reach, a step that can never complete, a gag wired to the wrong room, a boss that spawns somewhere odd. Co-op is part of it: everything that counts, moves, teleports or locks in players handles every present player (a bled-out spectator never holds a step up; nobody is left outside a boss fight), and every custom HUD line is per player unless it is meant for everyone (the boss waypoint). Checks: `Q4`, `Q5`; the manual pass is a test session with two or more players. *Judge:* the source excerpts and the facts together; report each as a finding with a severity.
- **P11 Story and a little mystery.** The map tells a coherent story through its dressing and text: names, dates, epitaphs, notes and set pieces connect to each other and to the quest (graves belong to people the story mentions; nothing is random filler that contradicts the lore). Leave room for mystery: optional lore, clues and small environmental puzzles the player pieces together, not everything spelled out. The main quest's current task is always named on screen, but it describes the challenge and leaves the where and how to the player (P17); mystery lives in optional lore and flavour too. *Judge:* pick five named things (graves, notes, signs); do they tie into one story? Is there at least one optional discovery that rewards looking closely, without blocking the required quest?
- **P12 Finished means finished.** When a quest step or event completes, everything that belonged to it stops: its loops, calls, timers, countdown lines, prompts and spawns (a DMV map kept calling ticket numbers after Take a Number was done). Anything a step calls out must be actionable by a player at that moment, never a dud (a called ticket number is one a player actually holds). *Judge:* for each step, find every thread and repeating message it starts and check it ends on the step's done notify; a loop with no end condition, or a prompt still usable after the step, fails. A penalty the player cannot avoid is a dud too: a missed call voids a ticket, so the call waits for the window's room; a score wipe on a timeout is said a round before it lands. This includes light: a glow that belongs to a shot, pressed or finished object goes out on the same notify (a looping effect played with `PlayFX` never stops; use a one-shot effect, an effect on a deletable `tag_origin`, or an exploder light; corn maze lanterns).
- **P13 Calm screen.** At most one message at a time where it matters: timed cues (a ticket call, a flash, a countdown) are never crowded by other lines, and there is no random ambient chatter that carries no information (random PA jokes made a map's ticket calls feel spammed and were removed). Flavour text is triggered by the player (a notice, a gag they caused), not by a timer; zone-entry lines and round-end radio/PA flavour are cut unless they carry something the player needs (constant broadcasts are noise; the broadcast half of P17). *Judge:* list every line printed on a timer, at random, at a round end or on entering a zone; each must carry information the player needs (and no location giveaway, P17), and gag lines must be held back while a timed cue is live.
- **P14 Things look like what they are.** A themed machine, buyable or quest object uses a model that matches its name and theme, never a stock stand-in that reads as something else (the DMV's speed perk "Vehicle Registration Fee" showed the Double Tap machine; it should be a car). Anything text refers to by name or number is visibly labelled in the world (the PA said "windows 1 to 4 are closed" but no window had a number, so the room read as one window). A boss must be unmistakable at a glance: a distinct model or colour and its name on screen, and it wears a themed outfit of its map (a boss that wore nothing themed read as an ordinary zombie; every boss wears two or three themed props, e.g. the Examiner's clock head, clipboard and OFFICE badge, burns with the hellhound fire and glows red on head, chest and hips, carries a red skull marker, and is named with its health on the objective HUD; actors cannot be scaled, so size comes from the props). *Judge:* each perk machine, buy spot and quest object's model against its name; each place or number named in a string against a visible label in that room. Each boss against a normal zombie beside it.
- **P15 Check how it actually looks.** Passing the checks is not enough: look at the map as a player sees it, from rendered views with real models, textures and lighting, before anyone plays it, and fix what looks wrong (queue stanchion posts stood with no belts between them, which no check caught; only a render showed it). What the views should confirm: every prop and grouping is something a real person would expect there, complete and doing its job. Assemblies are whole (posts have belts, desks have chairs facing them, shelves hold things that belong on shelves, lamps have somewhere to plug in or hang), objects sit where they'd be used, scale is right, and nothing is there only to raise a count. *Judge:* look at rendered views of each room (`zmforge render <recipe> --changed --compare latest`; `zmforge review` attaches them by default, `docs/developer-guide.md`, "How agents should use this") and ask of each cluster: what is it, why is it here, is anything missing or absurd? Without renders, read the prop table as groups and flag pieces that belong to an assembly whose partners are missing. Also flag, from past render passes: parts left behind by a dropped piece (sink legs with no sink), curtains with no rail, pipes or conduit that stop in mid-air, chairs not facing their desk; one wall module or picture repeated along a wall; litter spread evenly instead of in drifts; a plain board over a bare wall (it reads as a grey slab); a keep-out that bared the room behind a wall; an outdoor room with a flat wall, a black or hidden sky, nothing on the skyline, or one long empty walk. Against the stock look (map-design-method.md): every fire is a light pool with smoke and embers (`dressing.burning`), with dark gaps between pools; open air has effects (`S4`) and a skyline; walls and materials are weathered. Read each room's `art` block against its `stock` numbers. Do not fail a map for what the editor render cannot draw: perk machines, the box and scripted models, and preview-only colours.
- **P16 Simple enough to do while fighting.** Interactive choices must be understandable from a one-line prompt; no hidden or multi-step rules (a lever with a fee and a cooldown is too confusing). Every interaction is understood from its prompt alone (what pressing does and what you get) and done in one or two presses or a short walk; finding the thing and working out a challenge's method is the player's part (P17), because the player is being chased the whole time. No multi-step negotiations, no hidden state the player can't see (if a choice is remembered, say so on screen when it changes), no reading required mid-fight beyond one line. *Judge:* for each interactable, can a player who reads only its prompt know what pressing does and what they get, and finish it in a few seconds?
- **P17 Let players figure it out.** It is the culture of zombies maps to be a little confusing and to work things out. Objectives name the challenge in a short, flavourful line and do not spell it out ("Shoot out the jack-o'-lanterns", not "the glowing jack-o'-lanterns on the hay bales by the fence"); no text anywhere (objective, hint, notice, sign, lore, radio, PA) says where the power switch, Pack-a-Punch, perks, parts, buttons, quest items or secret steps are; no in-world maps, floor plans, diagrams or "you are here" boards that reveal the layout or where things are (a joke plan that is plainly wrong is fine); and few broadcasts, only ones that inform or react to the player. Kept: use prompts (`[{+activate}]` and what pressing does, P5), timers, progress counts, reset lines and timed cues (a FLASH line). Allowed, the one wayfinding exception: painted lightning-bolt symbols, the zombies convention, beside the doors on the cheapest route from the spawn toward power, on the side the player approaches from, pointing through the door. Never on the door into the power room, never within about 300 units of the switch (the last stretch is the player's to find), and never on doors off that route (beyond the power room, or leading back to it from deeper in). Every commit paints them (`zmforge.dressing.power_bolts`); a recipe opts out with `bp.power_bolts = False`. *Judge:* read every task, start, hint, notice, radio and PA line: a room or zone name used as directions, location words ("in the", "behind", "next to", "deepest", compass points), a count of where items are, or a method spelled out step by step warns; any in-world map of the real layout fails; a vague but clear objective passes. A bolt next to the power switch, or on a door off the spawn-to-power route, warns.
- **P18 Set pieces, pinch points, layered detail at three heights, light pools.** Outdoor areas read like a real, wrecked place, not open lanes between walls (from a Nuketown-style stock map: a derelict bus and a semi truck across the road, snapped poles and sagging wires, fire glowing in pools). Big set pieces (a wreck, a fallen tree, a collapsed silo) are both landmark and cover, and they squeeze the paths: no clean wide lane runs the length of an area. Every area carries detail at three heights: ground (dirt, grass, litter, stones), waist (fences, crates, bushes, debris) and overhead (wires, roofs, trees, a tower seen over the walls). Light comes in pools with real dark between them, from sources the player can see (a lantern, a fire, a window). Shared blocks: `dressing.outdoor` (`pinch_point`, `layered`, `light_pool_points`, `light_pools`), `dressing.backdrop` for what shows over a wall. *Judge:* in the renders of each outdoor room, name its set piece and where it narrows the path; count the layers you can see (all three, or which is missing); the widest straight clear run (over about 400 units of open lane with nothing in it fails); lamps against the 340 shadow ceiling and whether the pools have dark between them.
- **P19 GobbleGum on for a small game, off for a big one.** GobbleGum machines stay on with four or fewer players and switch off above four: the gate hides every machine and drops its trigger once a fifth player is in the game; a gum already chewed keeps working. Code: `script/gobblegum.py` (`MAX_BGB_PLAYERS`, `gobblegum_gate`). *Judge:* every map installs the gate; no recipe raises or removes `MAX_BGB_PLAYERS`.
- **P20 Power-locked first door.** Locking the very first door until the power is on is a valid pattern to send players to the power switch first, but the switch itself must be reachable from every starting zone with no purchase at all, or the map softlocks (cemetery). `Z80` walks the zone graph from spawn, treating a power-gated door as impassable, and fails when the only ways to the power switch's zone all cross one. *Judge:* the door graph's power-gated edges against the walk from spawn; a power-gated door is fine anywhere else on the route.
- **P21 Presentation images are the map itself plus its title.** The loading screen is the map's own top-down plan (the `planview` drawing: floors, prop footprints, spawns, doors, machines, starts), framed to fill the screen, with the title; the preview card and the Workshop thumbnail are an in-map photograph with the baked lighting (a `--baked` render, graded, with a fake haze when the map has fog) with the title. Never a generated illustration, figure or clip-art. `zmforge loadscreen <recipe>` makes all three; `generate` picks them up from `build-out/presentation/<map>/`.
- **P22 Every map has a gamble machine at its farthest point.** One machine per map: hold use to pay 2,000 for a 1 in 4 chance at 10,000, once per player per round, only when the player can pay. It stands against a wall at the spot with the longest *walk* from the spawn (the hub after a sealed start), in a room players can open, clear of lanes, interactives and doorways, lit by a small light pool, with its name (`LUCKY 4`) on the wall above. Code: `dressing/gamble.py` (placement and look, run by every `Project.commit`), `script/gamble.py` (the mechanic). *Judge:* `Z87` finds exactly one, reachable, charging only after `can_player_purchase`; test maps opt out with `bp.signature_gamble = False`.
- **P23 One recipe per feature, stock first.** Where a feature has a stock mechanism and a community one, the map uses one of them, the stock one where it exists, and never both: per-room ambience is `ambient_package` triggers baked by the sound converter, not the community `ambient_room` client script as well; a window's entrance tag sits on the barricade prefab or on its `exterior_goal` struct, consistently. An asset copied from a stock donor has what the donor carried reset (desaturation, colour tint, atlas column count, alias template). (from t7_companion) *Judge:* the recipe source and emitted files use one mechanism per feature; `Z92` holds the entrance-tag half.

<details><summary>Render rules, and where each is enforced</summary>

Each render rule below is a generic check or helper.

- Words hang or go on walls, never flat on the floor: `Z56`, `dressing.hanging_sign`.
- Props stay inside walls and on their surfaces: `Z54`, `dressing.fit_within`.
- Doorways and barricade windows stay clear: `Z53`, `Z57`.
- Models that render badly are deny-listed: `props/reads_badly.py`, `Z58`.
- Whole assemblies, litter in drifts, varied wall modules: `dressing.hang_bar`, `dressing.drift_points`, `dressing.no_repeat`, and the P15 Judge note.
- Keep-outs stop at the wall behind a machine; no plain boards over bare walls; know the preview's limits: P15 Judge note.
- Open-air walls dressed (piers, coping, plinth, railing, foot props) and low enough to show the sky, with silhouettes beyond: `dressing.boundary_wall`, `dressing.backdrop`, `add_room(wall_height=)`, checks `Z62`/`Z63`. Long walks narrowed and roofed by set pieces, brush dressing in grey stone not tan: P15 Judge note.

<details><summary>Build and engine principles (from t7_companion), and where each is enforced</summary>

These are about how a map is built and checked, not how it plays, so the review does not grade them per room.

- **Prove the build saw it.** A stage that fails silently is checked by what it wrote (the compiled entity text, `ambientgeometry.json`'s `Triggers`, the linker's `.deps` and the `.ff`), not by the source looking right. Enforced: the stage checks; `Z92` rests on the compiled `.d3dbsp`.
- **Silence is not a pass.** On a usermap Treyarch's asserts do not run, so a rule the stock scripts only guard with an assert needs a static check before the build: `Z86` (a respawn point sits in the zone its `script_noteworthy` names), `Z92` (spawn names a declared entrance).
- **Read the newest session.** A console log can hold several sessions or be from the previous run; count only from the last `Game Initialization`. See `how-zombies-maps-work.md`, "Asserts are silent on a usermap".
- **Instrument, then fix.** When a stack trace is all stock, mark each candidate line with an on-screen print and let one run say which ran, instead of fixing a guessed cause.
- **Effects belong to the client.** Effects and sounds are driven by clientfields, one-shot events use `counter` fields, and the effect precache budget (256 server, 1024 client) is spent deliberately `[reported]`. No check: the limit is unverified here.

</details>

<details><summary>How the review works and where its output goes</summary>

- `zmforge review <recipe>` (recipes: `dmv`, `cemetery`, `corn_maze`) generates the map into a scratch directory, measures it, renders each room, and writes a review packet with one call per room and one for the whole map; no model is called. The reviewer writes one findings file per call and `zmforge review --import <packet>` grades them.
- Each room call gets that room's props, fixtures, notices, quest points, scorecard and the recipe code that dresses it; the map call gets the essentials, door graph, quest, every string, the check results, the per-room grades and the quest/gag source.
- Output: the packet `build-out/review/<map>-<timestamp>.packet/` (each call's prompt, schema and findings), then, after `--import`, `build-out/review/<map>-<timestamp>.json` (everything) and `.md` (short report).
- `--no-ai` stops after the measurements; `--import <packet> --strict` exits non-zero when any principle fails; a build script can run the review before it builds, either as advisory output or, with `--strict`, as a gate that blocks the build on a fail.
- Thresholds (what "hugs the wall" means, the density floor, the engine ceilings) are constants at the top of `src/zmforge/review/facts.py`.

</details>
